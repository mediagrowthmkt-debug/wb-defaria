## The short answer: what a kitchen remodel costs in Massachusetts

For a typical single-family or condo kitchen in Essex and Middlesex County, most 2026 projects land in these ranges:

- **Minor / cosmetic refresh:** roughly $18,000 to $30,000. New countertops, cabinet refacing or repainting, updated hardware, backsplash, sink and faucet, some lighting. Layout stays the same.
- **Mid-range full remodel:** roughly $35,000 to $65,000. New cabinets, quartz or granite counters, new appliances, flooring, lighting, minor plumbing and electrical moves, full paint. The most common project we scope on the North Shore.
- **High-end / full gut with layout change:** $70,000 to $120,000 and up. Walls moved, structural work, island added, high-end appliances and custom cabinetry, plumbing and electrical relocated.

Those are Massachusetts numbers, not national averages. Labor and permitting in the greater Boston area run higher than the U.S. midpoint, which is exactly why a "$25,000 kitchen" you saw on a national blog rarely holds up once it is bid locally.

The honest truth every good contractor knows: the same kitchen can cost $40,000 or $75,000 depending on decisions you have not made yet. The estimate is not a guess. It is a map of those decisions.

## Where the money actually goes

Homeowners often assume the biggest line item is labor. It usually is not. Here is roughly how a mid-range Massachusetts kitchen remodel breaks down:

- **Cabinetry: 30% to 40%.** The single largest cost. Stock, semi-custom and full custom are three very different price worlds. This is the decision that moves your budget the most.
- **Countertops: 8% to 12%.** Laminate, butcher block, quartz and natural stone span a wide range. Quartz is the most requested in our area for its durability and low upkeep.
- **Labor: 15% to 25%.** Demolition, installation, finish carpentry and coordination. Skilled labor in the North Shore is not where you want to cut corners.
- **Appliances: 10% to 15%.** Depends entirely on the line you choose. A package can be $4,000 or $20,000.
- **Plumbing and electrical: 8% to 12%.** Goes up fast the moment you move a sink, add an island, or bring wiring up to current code.
- **Flooring, lighting, paint, backsplash, hardware: the remaining 10% to 15%.** The details that make the room feel finished.

Notice what that list tells you: two kitchens the same size can be $30,000 apart before anyone picks up a hammer. The gap is in the materials and the layout, not the crew.

## The line items that quietly blow up a budget

This is the part most estimates leave out on purpose. These are the costs that turn a "$50,000 kitchen" into a "$68,000 kitchen" once the project is underway, and every one of them is predictable if someone tells you upfront.

- **Moving the plumbing or the sink.** Relocating a sink or dishwasher means new supply and drain lines. Fine to do, expensive to discover mid-project.
- **Electrical to code.** Older North Shore homes, and there are a lot of them in Salem, Lynn and Beverly, often need updated wiring, GFCI outlets and a heavier circuit for a new range or island. An inspector will not let this slide.
- **What is behind the walls.** Once demolition opens things up, water damage, old knob-and-tube wiring, or a wall you thought was non-load-bearing can change the plan. Nobody can see through drywall, but a contractor who has done this in these towns knows what to expect.
- **Permits.** Kitchen remodels in Massachusetts almost always require a building permit, and often electrical and plumbing permits too. Skipping them is a problem you inherit when you sell the house.
- **Structural changes.** Taking down a wall to open the kitchen to the living room can mean a beam and an engineer's sign-off. That is a real number that should be in the estimate, not a surprise.

A contractor who has walked hundreds of North Shore kitchens can flag most of these on the first visit. That is the difference between an estimate and a wish.

## How to budget smart for a North Shore kitchen

A few things that consistently save homeowners money without cutting quality:

1. **Keep the layout if you can.** Not moving plumbing and gas lines is the single biggest way to control cost.
2. **Spend on what you touch every day.** Cabinets, counters and hardware are worth the investment. You can save on decorative extras.
3. **Choose semi-custom cabinets.** They deliver most of the custom look for a real price difference over full custom.
4. **Plan in the off-season.** Winter is when many North Shore homeowners plan interior work. Getting scoped in the slower months means a calmer timeline and a crew that is not stretched thin.
5. **Get a detailed estimate, not a range.** A one-line "$40k to $60k" tells you nothing. A real estimate is your best protection against overspending.

## FAQ

**How much does a kitchen remodel cost in Massachusetts in 2026?**
Most kitchen remodels in Essex and Middlesex County fall between $35,000 and $65,000 for a full mid-range remodel. Cosmetic refreshes start around $18,000, and full gut renovations with layout changes can reach $120,000 or more. Local labor and permitting run above the national average, so estimates should always be priced for the North Shore, not from a national calculator.

**What is the most expensive part of a kitchen remodel?**
Cabinetry, which typically accounts for 30% to 40% of the total budget. The choice between stock, semi-custom and custom cabinets moves your final number more than any other single decision.

**Do I need a permit to remodel my kitchen in Massachusetts?**
In almost all cases, yes. Massachusetts requires a building permit for most kitchen remodels, and separate electrical and plumbing permits when those systems are altered. A licensed contractor pulls these for you, and unpermitted work can create problems when you sell the home.

**How long does a kitchen remodel take?**
A typical full kitchen remodel runs six to ten weeks once work begins, depending on scope, material lead times and whether structural or plumbing changes are involved. A clear schedule should be part of your estimate from the start.

**How can I keep my kitchen remodel on budget?**
Keep the existing layout when possible, choose semi-custom cabinets, invest in the surfaces you use daily, and insist on a detailed line-item estimate instead of a vague range. The clearer the estimate, the fewer surprises during the build.
