Building up

Second-story and dormer additions on Saugus homes

Going up is often the best value on a small Saugus lot, because you add space without using the yard or the driveway. It is also the most structural kind of addition. Before design, we check whether the existing foundation, the first-floor walls and the beams can carry a new floor, and where new posts or footings are needed inside the house.

A full dormer on a cape keeps most of the existing roof and is usually less disruptive than a full second story. A full second story on a ranch means removing the roof, so we plan that stage around the weather: the roof comes off in sections, the new floor and walls go up quickly, and the exposed areas are sealed with membrane every night. Inside, the new stair has to land on the first floor, so we plan the stair, the bathroom plumbing stack and the heating and cooling for the upper floor together.

On our home additions page, rooms over the garage and second-story additions run about $150,000 to $400,000 or more, with structure, stairs and roof removal driving most of the cost.

Over the garage

Adding a room over the garage in Saugus

If your Saugus home has an attached garage with a flat or low roof, that footprint may be the easiest place to add a bedroom, office or playroom. The foundation and the floor already exist, so the budget goes into framing, the roof tie-in and the finish rather than excavation.

There are a few things that have to be done right. Garage ceiling joists are usually sized for storage, so the floor is reinforced or reframed to carry a real room. The ceiling between the car and the new room needs fire-rated drywall and a tight seal so exhaust never reaches the living space. The new floor sits over an unheated space, so it needs full insulation and air sealing or it will feel cold all winter.

You can see what this looks like in our own work: the bonus room over the garage with a vaulted ceiling shown on our home additions page is a real DeFaria project in Groton. We use the same approach on Saugus homes, matching the new roof and siding so the garage addition reads as part of the original house.

ADUs and in-law suites

In-law suites and ADUs in Saugus under the 2025 Massachusetts ADU law

Since February 2, 2025, the Massachusetts Affordable Homes Act allows one accessory dwelling unit by right on a lot with a single-family home in most of the state. The state limit is 900 square feet or half of the gross floor area of the main house, whichever is smaller, and towns cannot require the owner to live on site. That gives Saugus families a real option when they want a parent close by, or an adult child with their own front door.

An ADU can be attached, like a rear wing or a suite over the garage, or detached. It still has to meet local dimensional rules such as setbacks and height, plus the building code, fire separation and egress. The permit runs through the Town of Saugus Building Department, so we confirm with the town how its zoning applies to your lot before design starts.

For an in-law suite, we design around how the person will actually live: a step-free entry, a bathroom with a curbless shower and grab-bar blocking in the walls, a small kitchen, and good sound separation from the main house.

Rivers and marsh

Saugus River, Rumney Marsh and flood zone checks before you add on

Saugus has a lot of water. The Saugus and Pines Rivers and the Rumney Marsh run along the east side of town, and a large area around them is a state-designated Area of Critical Environmental Concern, defined by the 100-year flood elevation around the rivers and marsh. Homes in East Saugus and other low areas can sit close to that line even when the yard looks dry.

Under the Massachusetts Wetlands Protection Act, work within 100 feet of a wetland or inside a flood zone generally needs review by the Saugus Conservation Commission before the building permit. Depending on the site, that review can be a Request for Determination of Applicability or a full Notice of Intent with an Order of Conditions. If the lot is in a FEMA flood zone, the foundation height and construction of the addition can also change.

We check the flood map and the distance to wetlands at the first walkthrough. Finding this early keeps you from paying for plans that have to be redrawn, and it lets us build the approval time into the schedule instead of discovering it in the middle of the project.

Code and licenses

Massachusetts code, licenses and energy rules for a Saugus addition

A home addition in Massachusetts is built under the state building code, with separate electrical, plumbing and gas permits pulled by licensed tradespeople. Structural work on a house is supervised by a holder of a Massachusetts Construction Supervisor License, and contractors doing home improvement work on an owner-occupied home are required to be registered as a Home Improvement Contractor. Before you sign with anyone, ask for those numbers and for proof of insurance.

The new space also has to meet the Massachusetts energy code. In practice that means thicker insulation in the walls and roof, careful air sealing, better windows, and in many cases a blower door test at the end. For you it means an addition that is quieter, more even in temperature and cheaper to heat than the older part of the house.

Heating and cooling deserve their own conversation. Many Saugus additions are too far from the existing boiler or furnace to heat well with an extension of the old system, so a ductless heat pump is often the cleanest answer for the new rooms.

Winter planning

Planning a Saugus home addition around the New England winter

The calendar for an addition is set by design, engineering and permits as much as by construction. On our home additions page, a single-story family room or suite usually takes about three to five months to build once the permit is issued, and a room over the garage or a second story about four to seven months.

Foundation work and roof removal go best in good weather, so the smartest plan for most Saugus homeowners is to design and permit in the fall and winter and break ground in spring. Once the addition is framed, roofed and sealed, interior work like electrical, plumbing, insulation and drywall continues through the cold months without trouble.

Logistics matter on small Saugus lots. We plan where the dumpster and the material deliveries go, how the crew parks without blocking neighbors, and how to protect the driveway and lawn. How to compare home addition estimates in Saugus
