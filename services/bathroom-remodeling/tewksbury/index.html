Small bathrooms

Getting more out of a compact Tewksbury bathroom

Many Tewksbury homes, from ranches and capes to split-levels, have a hall bathroom that is smaller than the family needs. Moving walls is not always necessary. A wall-hung or floating vanity shows more floor and makes the room read larger, a recessed medicine cabinet adds storage without sticking into the room, and a pocket door frees the space a swinging door uses. Running one floor tile into a low-curb or curbless shower removes the visual break that makes a small bathroom feel chopped up.

Lighting matters as much as square footage. A ceiling light, light at the mirror at face height and a light in the shower remove the shadows that make a compact room feel cramped. A frameless glass panel instead of a curtain lets the whole room be seen at once.

Older homes

What we often find when opening walls in older Tewksbury homes

Demolition shows what the room has been hiding. In older homes it is common to find soft subfloor around the toilet or tub, old galvanized or mixed supply lines, drains without proper venting, an exhaust fan that vents into the attic instead of outside, and wiring that does not meet current code. None of these are reasons to panic, but they need to be fixed before new tile goes in.

We point out the most likely issues during the walkthrough, include reasonable allowances in the written estimate, and show you photos and a written change order before any extra work is done. You approve it first, so there are no surprises on the final bill.

Ventilation

Ventilation that protects your new bathroom

Moisture is what shortens the life of a bathroom. A properly sized exhaust fan, ducted to the outside and placed near the shower, removes steam before it soaks into drywall, paint and grout. A humidity-sensing or timer switch makes sure the fan runs long enough after a shower, even when someone forgets to turn it on.

Behind the tile, a continuous waterproofing system on the walls and the shower floor keeps water out of the framing. Good ventilation and good waterproofing together are what keep a remodeled bathroom looking new for years instead of a few seasons.

Accessibility

Planning a Tewksbury bathroom you can use for years

A remodel is the easiest time to make a bathroom safer for the long term, even if nobody in the house needs it today. Solid blocking inside the walls lets grab bars go in later without opening the tile. A low-curb or curbless shower, a handheld shower head on a slide bar, a built-in bench and a comfort-height toilet make the room easier for every age, and slip-resistant floor tile adds safety when the floor is wet.

Lever handles instead of knobs, a wider doorway where the framing allows it and good lighting at the entry are small choices that matter more as time goes on. When they are planned from the start, they look like good design, not like equipment.

Materials

Choosing tile, vanity and fixtures that last in Tewksbury

Porcelain tile holds up well on floors and shower walls and is easy to clean. Larger tiles mean fewer grout lines, and a quality grout, sealed where needed, keeps the shower easier to maintain. Choosing faucets and valves from brands with parts that are easy to find makes future repairs simple.
