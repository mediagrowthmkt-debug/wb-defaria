Waterproofing

Waterproofing comes before the tile

Tile and grout are not waterproof. A durable shower starts with a proper backer, a continuous waterproofing membrane over the walls, niche and bench, and a base sloped to the drain. Skipping this step is the most common reason a bathroom fails within a few years.

Ventilation is part of the same plan. A fan sized for the room and ducted outside, not into the attic, protects every other dollar spent on the bathroom, especially in older Rowley houses where moisture has nowhere else to go in winter.

What to expect while your Rowley bathroom is remodeled

Demolition and protection come first, then framing and floor repairs, plumbing and electrical rough-in with inspections, waterproofing and backer board, tile, the vanity and fixtures, glass, paint and a final walkthrough. Choosing tile, the vanity and fixtures before demolition keeps the crew from waiting on materials. Older homes

What we often find behind the walls in Rowley homes

Older Rowley houses often hide conditions that a quick quote never mentions: old supply lines, soft subfloor around the tub or toilet, wiring without ground-fault protection near water and fans that vent into the attic. When the walkthrough shows the signs we price the likely repairs up front, and anything new found once the walls are open is explained with photos and a price before extra work is approved.
