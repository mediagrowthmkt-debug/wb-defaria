Stair halls

Painting tall stair halls and entry halls in Winchester Victorians

The entry and stair hall of a Winchester Victorian is often the tallest painted space in the house, with a long run of wall, a staircase with turned balusters and a ceiling that cannot be reached from a normal ladder. It is also the first room guests see, so it is worth doing right.

We build proper staging over the stairs, protect the treads and the rail, and paint from the top down: ceiling, then the high walls, then the lower walls, trim and balusters by hand. Old plaster in these halls often has long settlement cracks along the stair line, which are taped and filled before painting so they do not reappear the first winter.

  • Staging built over the stairs for safe access
  • Settlement cracks along the stair line taped and filled
  • Balusters and rail painted by hand
  • Ceiling to floor, top down

Original woodwork

Original woodwork in Winchester homes: paint, strip or keep it stained?

Some Winchester Victorians still have original stained woodwork, while others have trim that has been painted many times. If the wood is still stained, painting it is a permanent choice, so we talk about it first. If it has already been painted, the usual goal is to keep the profiles sharp instead of adding more layers that soften the detail.

Where old paint is thick, cracked or peeling, it is removed with lead-safe methods before priming. Stripping trim back to bare wood is possible but slow and costly, so it is usually saved for the most visible pieces, such as a front door, a mantel or the newel post.

  • Stained wood kept or painted only after a test
  • Thick, failing paint removed with lead-safe methods

Wallpaper

Wallpaper removal before painting a Winchester room

Many Winchester homes still have wallpaper from past decades, sometimes two or three layers deep, and sometimes hung straight over unprimed plaster or drywall. Painting over it is a shortcut that shows: seams telegraph through the paint and the paper can bubble when it gets damp.

We score and steam or soak the paper, scrape it off, wash away the old paste, repair the surface underneath and seal it with a primer made for that job. When the paper was hung on raw drywall and the face tears, a skim coat brings the wall back to smooth before painting.

  • Paper scored, steamed or soaked and scraped.
  • Old paste washed off the wall.
  • Torn drywall face sealed and skim coated.
  • Primer chosen for walls that held wallpaper.

Painting a finished basement or lower level in Winchester

Lower levels in Winchester homes are often darker and cooler than the rest of the house, so color and light reflectance matter even more. Light, warm colors with a higher light reflectance value make a basement family room feel bigger.

Trim paint

Oil, latex or waterborne enamel for trim and doors

Trim and doors take the most wear in a Winchester house, so the paint has to be hard and washable. We check whether the existing paint is oil or latex, prime with a bonding primer where needed, and finish with a durable enamel.

Waterborne enamels dry harder than standard wall paint, level out to hide brush strokes and do not yellow the way old oil paint does over time.
