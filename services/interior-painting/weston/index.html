Fine finishes

Paneled libraries, wainscoting and detailed millwork in Weston homes

Weston homes often have more millwork than a typical house: paneled libraries and studies, wainscoting in dining rooms, built-in bookcases, coffered ceilings and long runs of crown. Painting these surfaces is closer to furniture finishing than wall painting. Every panel edge is sanded, every joint is caulked where it should be and left crisp where it should not, and the enamel is applied in thin coats so it levels without runs.

Stained woodwork is a separate decision. Some owners want to keep a natural library or a stained staircase rail and paint around it; others want to paint it for a lighter look. Painting stained wood is permanent in practice, so we test a hidden spot and talk through the result before committing.

Where the finish level is high, we also recommend a written color and sheen schedule for the whole house, with one ceiling white and one trim white, so rooms painted weeks apart still match perfectly.

  • Paneling and millwork sanded, caulked and enameled in thin coats
  • Stained wood tested before any decision to paint it

Wallpaper

Wallpaper removal before painting a Weston room

Old wallpaper is one of the most common surprises in Weston bedrooms, baths and dining rooms. Removing it properly takes longer than painting, so it is priced as its own line in the estimate.

After removal, any remaining adhesive is washed off, because paint over paste can crack and peel. Torn drywall paper is sealed and skimmed, and plaster is patched where the old paper pulled it loose.

Wall repair

Drywall and plaster repair: patch, skim coat or replace?

A Weston painting estimate always says how each wall will be repaired, because the repair decides how the finished room looks. Hairline cracks get taped and filled; a wall covered in old patches gets a skim coat; plaster that sags or sounds hollow is re-secured before anything else.

We would rather spend an extra day on a skim coat than paint over a wall that will crack again in the first winter.

  • Hairline cracks taped and filled.
  • Skim coat on walls with many patches or torn paper.
  • Loose plaster re-secured to the lath.
  • Damaged sections replaced and feathered in.

Trim paint

Oil, latex or waterborne enamel for trim and doors

Trim and doors take the most wear in a Weston house, so the paint has to be hard and washable. We check whether the existing paint is oil or latex, prime with a bonding primer where needed, and finish with a durable enamel.

Waterborne enamels dry harder than standard wall paint, level out to hide brush strokes and do not yellow the way old oil paint does over time.
