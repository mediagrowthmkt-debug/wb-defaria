Older homes

Painting older Gloucester homes: plaster, lead paint and oil-based trim

Gloucester ranges from a dense harbor core to outlying Cape Ann villages of 18th- and 19th-century maritime cottages and waterfront homes. That brings three things to plan for before painting: plaster that cracks and loosens with age, lead in paint applied before 1978, and trim that was finished with oil paint decades ago.

Loose plaster is re-secured and taped so cracks do not come back through the new paint. In homes built before 1978, sanding or scraping old paint requires lead-safe work practices under federal and Massachusetts rules, with containment and careful cleanup. Latex over old oil trim peels unless the trim is cleaned, scuffed and primed with a bonding primer first.

  • Plaster cracks re-secured and taped before painting.
  • Lead-safe prep in homes built before 1978.
  • Bonding primer over old oil-based trim.
  • Profiles and detail on old trim kept crisp, not buried.

Wallpaper

Wallpaper removal before painting a Gloucester room

Many Gloucester homes still have wallpaper from past decades, sometimes two or three layers deep, and sometimes hung straight over unprimed plaster or drywall. Painting over it is a shortcut that shows: seams telegraph through the paint and the paper can bubble when it gets damp.

We score and steam or soak the paper, scrape it off, wash away the old paste, repair the surface underneath and seal it with a primer made for that job. When the paper was hung on raw drywall and the face tears, a skim coat brings the wall back to smooth before painting.

  • Paper scored, steamed or soaked and scraped.
  • Old paste washed off the wall.
  • Torn drywall face sealed and skim coated.
  • Primer chosen for walls that held wallpaper.

Basements

Painting a finished basement or lower level in Gloucester

Lower levels in Gloucester homes are often darker and cooler than the rest of the house, so color and light reflectance matter even more. Light, warm colors with a higher light reflectance value make a basement family room feel bigger.

Drywall in a finished basement is painted like any other room. Bare masonry is different: it needs to be dry, clean and free of efflorescence, and it takes a masonry primer or coating made for concrete. If the wall is damp, the moisture is solved first.

Dark colors

Accent walls, dark colors and how many coats they need

Deep colors are popular in dining rooms, offices and powder rooms, but they need more planning. A tinted primer under a dark color saves a coat, and most deep colors still need two full finish coats to look even.

Going from dark back to light also takes extra coats, which is why a dramatic color change moves the price. We note the number of coats per room in the estimate so there are no surprises.

After painting

Caring for fresh paint after the job

Latex paint feels dry within hours but takes a few weeks to cure fully. During that time, avoid scrubbing walls and be gentle with furniture against them.

At the end of the job we leave labeled, sealed cans of each color and sheen, with the room written on the lid, so future touch-ups match.

Wall repair

Drywall and plaster repair: patch, skim coat or replace?

A Gloucester painting estimate always says how each wall will be repaired, because the repair decides how the finished room looks. Hairline cracks get taped and filled; a wall covered in old patches gets a skim coat; plaster that sags or sounds hollow is re-secured before anything else.

We would rather spend an extra day on a skim coat than paint over a wall that will crack again in the first winter.

  • Hairline cracks taped and filled.
  • Skim coat on walls with many patches or torn paper.
  • Loose plaster re-secured to the lath.
  • Damaged sections replaced and feathered in.

Application

Brush, roller or spray: how the paint goes on

Most interior walls and ceilings are cut in by brush at the edges and rolled in the field, which gives an even texture and lets the crew work while the family is home. Spraying is faster on empty rooms, new construction and cabinet doors, but it needs full masking and time to set up.

Trim and doors are usually brushed with a leveling enamel that flows out and hides brush marks. Cabinet doors are often sprayed off site or in a protected area for a smooth factory-like finish.

  • Cut in by brush, rolled in the field on walls.
  • Leveling enamel brushed on trim and doors.
  • Spray for cabinets and empty rooms.
  • Two finish coats over a primed surface.

Trim paint

Oil, latex or waterborne enamel for trim and doors

Trim and doors take the most wear in a Gloucester house, so the paint has to be hard and washable. We check whether the existing paint is oil or latex, prime with a bonding primer where needed, and finish with a durable enamel.

Waterborne enamels dry harder than standard wall paint, level out to hide brush strokes and do not yellow the way old oil paint does over time.
