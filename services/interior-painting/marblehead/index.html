Wallpaper

Wallpaper removal before painting a Marblehead room

Many Marblehead homes still have wallpaper from past decades, sometimes two or three layers deep, and sometimes hung straight over unprimed plaster or drywall. Painting over it is a shortcut that shows: seams telegraph through the paint and the paper can bubble when it gets damp.

We score and steam or soak the paper, scrape it off, wash away the old paste, repair the surface underneath and seal it with a primer made for that job. When the paper was hung on raw drywall and the face tears, a skim coat brings the wall back to smooth before painting.

  • Paper scored, steamed or soaked and scraped.
  • Old paste washed off the wall.
  • Torn drywall face sealed and skim coated.
  • Primer chosen for walls that held wallpaper.

Painting kitchens and bathrooms in Marblehead homes

Dark colors

A Marblehead painting estimate always says how each wall will be repaired, because the repair decides how the finished room looks. Hairline cracks get taped and filled; a wall covered in old patches gets a skim coat; plaster that sags or sounds hollow is re-secured before anything else.

Application

Brush, roller or spray: how the paint goes on

Most interior walls and ceilings are cut in by brush at the edges and rolled in the field, which gives an even texture and lets the crew work while the family is home. Spraying is faster on empty rooms, new construction and cabinet doors, but it needs full masking and time to set up.

Trim and doors are usually brushed with a leveling enamel that flows out and hides brush marks. Cabinet doors are often sprayed off site or in a protected area for a smooth factory-like finish.

  • Cut in by brush, rolled in the field on walls.
  • Leveling enamel brushed on trim and doors.
  • Spray for cabinets and empty rooms.
  • Two finish coats over a primed surface.

Trim paint

Oil, latex or waterborne enamel for trim and doors

Trim and doors take the most wear in a Marblehead house, so the paint has to be hard and washable. We check whether the existing paint is oil or latex, prime with a bonding primer where needed, and finish with a durable enamel.

Waterborne enamels dry harder than standard wall paint, level out to hide brush strokes and do not yellow the way old oil paint does over time.
